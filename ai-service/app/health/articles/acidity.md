Acidity, or heartburn, is a burning feeling in the chest or upper stomach, sometimes with a sour taste or burping. It happens when stomach acid comes back up towards the throat. It is common and often gets better with changes to eating habits.

## Often linked with
- Large meals, or eating late at night
- Spicy, oily or fried food
- Tea, coffee, fizzy drinks or alcohol
- Lying down soon after eating
- Irregular meal times, or long gaps between meals
- Smoking, stress, being overweight, or pregnancy
- Some medicines, including some painkillers

## What may help
- Eat smaller meals more often, and don't skip meals
- Have your last meal 2 to 3 hours before you lie down
- Cut down on spicy, oily and fried food
- Cut down on tea, coffee, fizzy drinks and alcohol
- Drink water through the day
- Raise the head end of your bed a little if it is worse at night
- Stop smoking, and keep to a healthy weight
- Ask a pharmacist about a suitable remedy, and tell them about any other medicines you take

## See a doctor if
- It keeps coming back, or you have it on most days for 3 weeks or more
- Remedies from a pharmacy haven't helped
- You are over 55 and it is new for you
- You often feel sick or vomit, or you are losing weight without trying
- You also have stomach pain that doesn't go away

## Get urgent help if
- You vomit blood, or vomit that looks like coffee grounds
- Your stools are black, sticky or tar-like
- You have chest pain that spreads to your arm, neck, jaw or back, or comes with sweating or breathlessness
- Food gets stuck, or swallowing is painful or difficult
