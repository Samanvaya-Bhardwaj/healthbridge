Trouble sleeping is common and is often linked to habits, stress or worry. Small changes to your routine usually help within a few weeks.

## Often linked with
- Stress, worry or low mood
- Irregular sleep times, or shift work
- Screens late at night
- Tea, coffee or alcohol in the evening
- A noisy, bright or hot bedroom
- Napping during the day
- Pain or other health problems

## What may help
- Go to bed and wake up at the same times every day
- Avoid screens for an hour before bed
- Have no tea or coffee after mid-afternoon, and avoid alcohol
- Keep your bedroom dark, quiet and cool
- Get daylight and some exercise during the day, but not right before bed
- Relax before bed, for example with reading, a warm bath or slow breathing
- If you can't sleep, get up and do something calm until you feel sleepy

## See a doctor if
- It has lasted for months, or is affecting your daily life
- Changes to your routine haven't helped
- You snore loudly, gasp, or stop breathing in your sleep
- You feel low, anxious or hopeless on most days

## Get urgent help if
- You have thoughts of harming yourself: call 112, or the Tele-MANAS helpline on 14416
