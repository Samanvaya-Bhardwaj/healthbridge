A fever is a body temperature of 38 °C (100.4 °F) or above. It is usually the body's response to an infection, and in adults it often settles within 2 to 3 days.

## Often linked with
- Viral infections such as colds and flu
- Other infections, for example of the throat, ear, chest or urine
- Mosquito-borne illnesses such as dengue and malaria, which are common in India
- Some vaccinations

## What may help
- Rest, and drink plenty of fluids so you don't get dehydrated
- Wear light clothing, and keep the room cool and airy
- A lukewarm sponge can make you more comfortable; avoid cold water
- Check your temperature with a thermometer and note it down
- Eat light food when you feel like it
- Ask a pharmacist or doctor about medicine to bring a fever down

## See a doctor if
- It lasts more than 3 days, or keeps coming back
- You also have a rash, body aches with a severe headache, or pain behind the eyes
- You have burning when passing urine, a cough that doesn't settle, or ear pain
- You have a long-term condition, are pregnant, or it is in an older person

## Get urgent help if
- A baby under 3 months has a temperature of 38 °C or above
- There is a stiff neck, a severe headache, or a rash that doesn't fade when you press a glass on it
- There is confusion, unusual drowsiness or a fit (seizure)
- There is difficulty breathing
- There is bleeding from the gums or nose, or repeated vomiting
