Diarrhoea means loose or watery stools, more often than usual. It is often caused by a stomach infection and usually gets better within a few days. The main risk is losing too much water from the body (dehydration).

## Often linked with
- A stomach infection from food or water that was not clean
- Food that was not stored or cooked properly
- Travel
- Some medicines, such as antibiotics
- Stress or anxiety

## What may help
- Drink plenty of fluids, little and often, such as water, clear soups, coconut water or buttermilk
- Use oral rehydration solution (ORS) from a pharmacy if you are passing many loose stools
- Eat light food when you feel like it, such as rice, khichdi, curd rice, bananas or toast
- Wash your hands with soap after the toilet and before eating
- Drink safe water, such as boiled or filtered water
- Rest

## See a doctor if
- It lasts more than 2 to 3 days, or keeps coming back
- You can't keep fluids down
- You have a high fever
- It is in a baby, a young child, an older person, or someone who is pregnant or has a long-term illness

## Get urgent help if
- You have signs of dehydration: very little or no urine, dizziness, a very dry mouth or sunken eyes
- There is blood in your stools, or your stools are black
- You have severe stomach pain
- You feel very drowsy or confused
