Indigestion is discomfort or fullness in the upper stomach during or after eating, often with gas, bloating or burping. It is common and is often linked with eating habits.

## Often linked with
- Eating quickly, or eating large meals
- Fatty, spicy or very rich food
- Fizzy drinks, alcohol, tea or coffee
- Foods that make gas for some people, such as beans, cabbage or onions
- Milk products, if they don't suit you
- Stress or anxiety, and smoking

## What may help
- Eat slowly and chew your food well
- Eat smaller, regular meals
- Notice which foods bring it on, and have less of them
- Cut down on fizzy drinks and alcohol
- Take a gentle walk after meals rather than lying down
- Drink enough water, and keep active
- Ask a pharmacist about a suitable remedy

## See a doctor if
- It keeps coming back, or lasts more than a few weeks
- Remedies from a pharmacy haven't helped
- Your tummy stays bloated or swollen
- You are losing weight without trying, or don't feel like eating
- A change in your bowel habits lasts more than 3 weeks

## Get urgent help if
- You vomit blood, or your stools are black or tar-like
- You have severe stomach pain that comes on suddenly or keeps getting worse
- You have chest pain, especially with sweating, breathlessness or pain spreading to the arm or jaw
- You have difficulty swallowing
