Constipation means passing stools less often than usual, or stools that are hard, dry or difficult to pass. It is common and usually gets better with changes to food, drink and routine.

## Often linked with
- Not eating enough fibre from fruit, vegetables, whole grains and pulses
- Not drinking enough water
- Not being active
- Ignoring the urge to go, or a change in routine or travel
- Pregnancy
- Some medicines, such as some painkillers and iron tablets

## What may help
- Drink plenty of water through the day
- Eat more fibre, such as fruit, vegetables, whole grains, pulses and salads, adding it gradually
- Fruit such as prunes, figs or pears can help some people
- Keep active: a daily walk helps
- Go to the toilet when you feel the urge, and give yourself time
- Resting your feet on a low stool while on the toilet can make it easier
- Ask a pharmacist about a suitable remedy if these changes don't help

## See a doctor if
- It isn't getting better after about 2 weeks of these changes
- You are often constipated, or it is a new change that lasts
- You see blood in your stools
- You are losing weight without trying, or feel very tired
- You have bloating or stomach pain that doesn't go away

## Get urgent help if
- You can't pass stools or wind at all, and your tummy is swollen or you are vomiting
- You have severe stomach pain
- You pass a lot of blood, or your stools are black and tar-like
